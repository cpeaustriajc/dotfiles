# Filters and red flags

Read this before you score candidates. The steps are in `SKILL.md`.

Score each candidate yes, no or unknown on each line. For personal software the filters matter
less.

1. **People already pay** for this outcome, with money or with a workaround they put up with.
2. **A painkiller**: they would buy it this week. It can have a buy button on day one.
3. **I can reach them**: an audience, community, search pages, marketplace listing or sales
   motion I own or can build. If not, a copy with distribution wins.
4. **My edge is knowledge, not code**: I am the user, or I know the work from the inside.
5. **A moat a copy can't take quickly**: per-customer data or history, switching costs,
   hard integrations, trust, a licence or compliance record, a community, a brand.
6. **Small enough that big players won't bother**, big enough to pay for my time.
7. **Neither the buyer nor a model lab would build it.** A thin wrapper over open-source parts
   (buyers build it in a weekend) or over a single model call (labs absorb it) fails. Ongoing
   operations, infrastructure, compliance or taste pass.
8. **Worth it even if copied**: it pays back in weeks, grows my audience either way, or teaches
   me what I wanted to learn.

## Red flags

Say these plainly when a candidate shows them:

- A tool for other builders whose only channel is search, the channel shrinking fastest.
- A free tool or a waitlist as the plan to validate; validation is someone paying.
- Agents and automation before a single user.
- A complex product as the first one, when an add-on or a one-time sale would teach the same.
- Sharing a viral idea's numbers before owning its distribution: it invites the copies.
