# Is it worth building, and worth my time?

Read this for the worth-it branch of round 1. The steps are in `SKILL.md`.

Look up what you can before asking. Ask who it is for if that isn't plain, because the
questions differ:

| Question | For others | For me alone |
|---|---|---|
| **Real** | Who has the problem, how often, and what they do now. Rob Fitzpatrick, "The Mom Test" (2013): a workaround people pay for or put up with is evidence; "it would be nice" is not. | How often I hit it and the minutes it costs. Five minutes a day is about 30 hours a year: the budget to beat, if time saved is the point. |
| **Already solved** | Existing products, tools or a spreadsheet. Say what this does that they don't, or recommend one. | A tool 80% there with some setup, a script, a spreadsheet: an option to price, not a reason to stop. |
| **Cost** | My weeks to a first usable version, plus running and upkeep. Estimates are mine; label yours as guesses. | Evenings and weekends, plus upkeep when an API or the OS changes. |
| **Reach and copies** | How I reach the people who pay, and what a weekend copy built with an agent can't take: distribution I own, data that accrues per customer, a workflow it sits in, trust, compliance. If a copy with an audience would win, say so. | Not asked. |
| **Displaces** | Opportunity cost; I decide which matters more. | The same. |
| **Returns** | Money, time saved, learning or joy; say which. | Time saved, learning or joy. For learning or joy the time math doesn't apply, but the build gets a timebox ("two weekends") that ends in a keep-going or stop decision. |

## Verdict

Recommend one, with its reason in a line:

- **build**;
- **shrink**: a smaller first version is worth it, the whole isn't yet;
- **test first**: one cheap check decides it (a landing page, five conversations, a prototype);
- **don't build**, which ends the interview.

Test first and don't build are good outcomes; say them plainly when the evidence points there.
