# Aesthetic: what reads as amateur

Each rule names a failure from a real review of a real painting.

- **Name the tradition, then match it.** "Medieval" meant a Romantic academic oil, not a
  manuscript. Tsushima needed Japanese ink-wash and terrain, not Chinese needle peaks. Paint a real
  place in its own culture's tradition; confirm the tradition when the brief is only an era or place.
- **Amateur tells:** flat fills, even outline weight, primitive shapes, regular spacing (tree rows,
  dotted clouds, a uniform grid of lit windows), no value plan, no atmospheric depth, even detail
  everywhere. The fixes:
  - Masses before detail. Clouds are clusters with a flat base, a lit side and a shadow side.
  - A value plan: light and shadow families apart, the darkest dark and lightest light at the
    focal point.
  - Warm light, cool shadow.
  - Lost and found edges. A glow is a soft gradient, not a hard-edged translucent rectangle.
  - Organic irregularity: noise, clusters, overlaps, gaps. Night facade lights are sparse and
    clustered; accents go on a few edges, not around every shape.
  - A detail hierarchy: crisp near the subject, simplified elsewhere.
- **Materials.** Polished metal reflects an environment that matches the scene. A saturated red
  needs a deep, slightly cool shadow. Fine repeating texture (mail, windows) needs a pitch the
  medium keeps, or it smears into a slab.
- **The medium sets the smallest mark** (halftone dot, oil brush, ink bleed). Enlarge what must
  read, leave out what can't, and judge a zoomed crop of the stylised output, not the raw render.
- **Unify.** One light lights everything, figures included. A 3D figure looked painted only once
  the same medium pass covered it. Keep outline weights consistent.
- **A change of era or state swaps its objects.** Between eras, seasons or day and night, each
  object of one state (vehicles, signs, clothing, street furniture) gets its own version. A
  recoloured 2013 car still reads as 2013 in 2077. List them before building; crossfade.
