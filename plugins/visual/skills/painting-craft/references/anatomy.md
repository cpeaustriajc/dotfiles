# Anatomy and proportion: failures seen

Use the proportions in Andrew Loomis, "Figure Drawing for All It's Worth" (1943), and a 180 cm adult man, in centimetres. Replace with a canon or museum
source when one exists.

- **Measure** and cite each number. Eyeballed figures came out 1.6× too long, twice. No canon
  height: a named proxy (the actor's or mocap performer's height), stated.
- **Foreshortened poses are built as volume** (facing the viewer, seated, a limb toward camera):
  boxes and cylinders in perspective, overlapping where nearer; flat outlines only near profile.
  Check the pose as a bare mannequin before dressing it.
- **Limbs** of a 180 cm man: thigh 44, shin 42, upper arm 33, forearm 26, hip joint to shoulder
  joint about 50.
- **Layers grow outward**: a sleeve wider than its plate hides the plate; see `armour.md`.
- **Hands have mass.** About 40 px at painting scale: one mass, fingers touching, a clear cuff;
  thin separate fingers read as claws.
- **Reach:** target closer than 59 (upper arm + forearm). Place the hand, then the elbow: out and
  down for an arm across a knee, out and back for a hand on the thigh. Guessed angles leave the
  arm horizontal.
- **Sleeping head:** yaw within 5° of the torso, chin down 10-15°, loll 15-20°. Less looks awake;
  more sinks a helmet rim into the shoulder.
- **Cars** are judged as fast as a face; a body on two circles with no arches or shadow reads as a toy.
  Arch radius 1.2 × wheel radius, dark wheel well, flat contact shadow.

Past pictures' measurements: `worked-example.md`.

Ratios to measure at review, in pixels: helmet ÷ seated height 0.35 with a bascinet, 0.25 bare;
horse body ÷ withers 1.0; dog body ÷ withers 1.1; car length ÷ height about 3.5 (coupe), 4 (wedge).
